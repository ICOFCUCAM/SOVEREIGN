## 2 Executive overview

Northwind Analytics is a software-as-a-service business in vertical software, founded in 2016 and headquartered in the United Kingdom. It has $21.8M of annual recurring revenue, growing 36% year over year, and $24.6M of revenue over the last twelve months. It is EBITDA-positive at a 14% margin. Its shareholders are inviting a small number of qualified acquirers to consider a purchase of the Company through a structured, confidential process.

- Revenue (TTM): $24.6M
- ARR: $21.8M — 36% YoY growth
- Gross margin: 78%
- EBITDA margin: 14%
- Net retention: 117%

## 3 Investment highlights

The case for Northwind Analytics rests on the quality of its recurring revenue and its economics. Each point below is drawn from the figures in this memorandum or attributed to the Company.

- Recurring revenue of $21.8M ARR, up 36% on the prior year
- 78% gross margin; EBITDA-positive at a 14% margin
- Rule of 40: 50 (ARR growth 36% plus EBITDA margin 14%), above the 40 that buyers of recurring-revenue businesses commonly use as a threshold
- Net revenue retention of 117%: existing customers, taken together, spend more each year than the last
- A diversified customer base: the ten largest customers are 22% of revenue
- Network effects reported by the Company: the product becomes more useful as more customers use it
- A proprietary data asset reported by the Company, accumulated through use of the product

## 11 Process and timing

The Company is being offered through a targeted, confidential process: outreach to qualified candidates, then indications of interest, a letter of intent with exclusivity, confirmatory diligence and signing. All requests for information go through the process coordinator; recipients must not contact management, customers or employees directly. The dates below are a planning assumption, not a commitment; dates move with buyer response, diligence findings and conditions to closing.

- Weeks 1–2 · NDAs and CIM — Confidentiality agreements signed; the memorandum and staged data-room access released to signed parties
- Weeks 2–3 · Management presentations — Engaged buyers meet the management team
- Week 4 · Indications of interest — Non-binding proposals due from engaged buyers
- Weeks 5–6 · Letter of intent — Preferred party chosen; terms and exclusivity agreed in writing
- Weeks 6–9 · Confirmatory diligence — The preferred party completes its detailed review under exclusivity
- Weeks 8–10 · Definitive agreement — Sale and purchase terms negotiated and signed
