# Homepage narrative order (executive reading journey)

Do not delete. This is the canonical structure for the public homepage. A CIO,
Minister, Chief Justice, University Registrar or Secretary General reads in this
order, and each section should answer the next question naturally — like a
government white paper, a Bloomberg feature, or an Apple keynote, not a SaaS
product page.

The executive's questions, in order:
1. What is this?
2. Why does it matter?
3. Why can't we continue as we are?
4. How does it work?
5. Why is it different?
6. Can I trust it?
7. Can it work for us?
8. How do I evaluate it?

## Section order

1. **Hero** — large institutional image; "Governing the world's most important
   decisions." Immediately below: three proof statements (not paragraphs) —
   **Governed Workflows · Permanent Records · Independent Verification**.
2. **The Problem** — "Institutions don't pay to create documents. They pay to
   make them official." (why Dispatch exists). No numbered list.
3. **The Journey** — "Watch a draft become institutional authority." Animated:
   the document itself moves through the stages; the user sees governance happen.
4. **What makes an Official Record** — the white/light section (the output).
   Problem → Process → Output.
5. **Why governance matters** — "Institutions don't become trusted by
   publishing. They become trusted by governing publication." Premium feature
   panels, not an accordion. Explain *why* governance matters.
6. **Business value / ROI** — what the institution gains: lower cost, fewer
   errors, faster approvals, institutional memory, audit readiness, software
   consolidation, digital services. (The 7 value pages.)
7. **Trusted every day / Industries** — institutional scenarios, so an executive
   sees their own publication types:
   - Government: Executive Orders · Regulations · Gazettes
   - Justice: Judgments · Court Rules · Legal Notices
   - Healthcare: Clinical Directives · Safety Bulletins · Treatment Protocols
   - Universities: Senate Decisions · Degree Regulations · Academic Policies
   - Enterprise: Board Resolutions · Compliance Policies · Corporate Governance
   - Regulators: Licenses · Enforcement Decisions · Public Notices
   Answers: "Can this handle the publications my institution actually produces?"
8. **Sovereign architecture** — "Sovereign by design. Verifiable by default."
   Executives buy outcomes first; architects buy infrastructure — so this sits
   lower.
9. **Proof / Procurement** — Security, Compliance, Evidence, Procurement, API,
   Architecture. Near the end.
10. **Evaluate** — "Prove it using your own documents." / Launch Dispatch.

## Removed
- The numbered "Policy Draft → Legal Review → …" list on the right of the
  Problem section — it read like documentation. The animated journey (3) is the
  visual workflow; the list is redundant.

## One-line story
HERO → THE PROBLEM → THE JOURNEY → THE RESULT → WHY GOVERNANCE MATTERS →
BUSINESS VALUE → WHO USES IT → TRUST → EVALUATE.
